# Setting Up Your Space for Your Foster Pet & Guidelines for Kittens!

## Foster Space in the Home

Before you introduce a foster pet into your home, it is imperative to make sure that the space they will be living in is a safe and calm environment. Think of it as “babyproofing” your home for your new guest. This will help both you and your wonderful foster pet be as comfortable as possible! If you have an office or bonus space that is not being currently used, these are great options. If a separate room is not available, be sure that you use a separator to create an enclosement, particularly for kittens who should not be allowed to roam freely throughout a home. Make sure that it is a space that is separated from any other household pets. This is important because it will prevent the spread of disease between animals and will prevent distress between new pets entering the home. Additionally, foster pets should be kept adequately separated from any young children, and should only interact with said children when monitored by an adult. Be sure to consider if the space you plan to have your foster pets is a room where the temperature can be controlled. This can include AC, fans, heating system, or a heater, but be cautious when utilizing space heaters or other portable devices that could be hot to the touch.

As for the space itself, there’s a few things to consider to ensure the utmost safety. It is best to hide all electrical cords (especially for kittens), and consider covering all outlets. The space and surrounding areas should not have any chemicals or cleaning products out as they are toxic and can cause spillage. Look around the foster space and be sure there are no small items that could be ingested and cause blockages or be a choking hazard. This includes even items like foam balls that could fall apart and be ingested.

Prior to letting any furry new friends enter your home, make sure to disinfect. This can include most surfaces, but should at least include the foster space. Consider which cleaning solutions to use, as some can be toxic for cats if, for example, they step on a freshly sanitized area with their paws and then proceed to groom themselves with those paws. The Charleston Animal Society recommends a solution that is 1 part bleach and 32 parts water. 

For your own ease, try to find a foster space in your home with limited carpet and upholstery, particularly those that are difficult to clean as pets can be messy, and spaces should be cleaned frequently. Double check that all windows and doors can be closed all the way, foster cats should not be let outdoors! Also, be sure to close or seal off toilet lids as much as possible. 

Did you know? Some common plants and flowers are actually very toxic to cats. Non-safe common household plants include: lilies, sago palms, corn plant, aloe plant, asparagus fern. A comprehensive list of toxic and non-toxic plants for cats can be found on the [ASPCA website](https://www.aspca.org/pet-care/animal-poison-control/cats-plant-list).


## Given Supplies vs. What You May Want to Buy (Fostering on a Budget)

As a cat foster parent, you will be provided with:
  * A litter box
  * Litter
  * A litter scooper
  * Food (wet and dry)
  * Water and food bowls
  * Toys
  * A scratcher

If the shelter you are working with to foster pets refuses to provide these supplies without adequate reasoning, that may be an indication that they are not a great organization to foster from. You may, of course, want to purchase additional supplies. It is helpful to have plastic bags on hand for disposing of litter, as well as paper towels, rags, and spray bottles for cleaning and disinfecting. It is always fun to get new toys for your foster pet, just make sure these are being sanitized too. You will likely be provided with some towels and blankets, but consider having more on hand, and cat beds for them to sleep on can help make your foster cat feel extra cozy and safe. You may want to consider buying metal food bowls, as plastic or other bowls can cause acne and are more porous, therefore harder to clean. 

**Kittens!**

Additional supplies needed for kittens:

Given their small size and new-ness to the world, kittens can require additional supplies. These are usually a smaller litterbox and bowls that are more suitable for short legs to reach, kitten-specific wet and dry food, and in instances with very young kittens - syringes and milk formula. Kittens are very playful and usually full of energy, which is lots of fun, but also means they require more frequent attention than older cats. Be sure to give your kitten lots of play time and keep an eye on them as often as your schedule allows. On top of all of their energy, kittens need lots of sleep, so providing them with a cozy and relatively quiet space is key. Kittens are small and adventurous, meaning they are more likely to find themselves in small crevices or hiding spots you may not have previously considered. In addition to illnesses, kittens are more susceptible to temperature, so be sure to keep their space at a moderate temperature to ensure they do not get too cold or hot. In fact, kittens are better suited for warmth, so consider plugging in a heated mat with a blanket. 

In general, kittens above 2 weeks of age should be able to use the litterbox intuitively, though it is a good idea to introduce them to their litter box upon their arrival. Kittens who have been weaned, typically by week 3 or 4, should be able to drink water from a shallow dish. Kittens under these ages will likely need to be helped when it comes to using the bathroom (referred to as ‘stimulating) and being weaned off a bottle/their mother.

Kittens between the ages of 0-3 weeks should be fed and stimulated every 2-3 hours. Those that are 3-4 weeks should be fed and stimulated every 4 hours, 4-5 weeks every 5 hours while they begin to use the litterbox themselves. For the remainder of the weeks they are under your care you should feed them kitten-safe food every 6-8 hours. Kittens should progress from being bottle fed formula, to a mixture of wet food and water, until kitten-safe wet and dry food.
